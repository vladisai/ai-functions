# Preface

It has been said that “books, once written, have no need of their authors.” Nor do they need their readers,
at least not until they have been written. Even though a book may seem to connect authors and readers,
effectively it is the object that separates them. This book works differently. It is not written once and for
all. It writes itself as the reading progresses – individually for each reader. The more you already know,
the shorter your book. This book is not merely interactive, presenting a fixed body of knowledge differently
depending on input from the reader. It is what we call transductive: its content is instantiated at the time
of reading based on what is known at that particular moment to that particular reader. Read it again in
six months and you may get a different book — shorter in the places where you have grown, longer where
the subject matter has shifted. Since all knowledge is provisional, the book may at some point nudge you
to read new material needed to update your understanding.

This book draws on the time-varying body of publicly available knowledge, supplemented by whatever
the reader chooses to share: papers, code, notes, whether public or private to their working environment.
The outcome of reading is an artifact that captures the subject matter tailored to one person at one time,
that adapts over time by integrating into the reader’s digital knowledge base.

So, the book is both an agent and an evolving artifact. This is what we mean by an agentic book. It is
an educational agent whose job is to make you understand about the subject. Specifically this is an agentic
book about agents: how they are designed, built, and managed. But how does the book know you have
understood? And how do you know that the material it has crafted for you is sound? Understanding requires
alignment of language, which is an active process, and which leads to a different outcome for every reader.
Your engagement as a reader is an integral part of the writing process.

To keep the process flexible, the agent operates on the principle that readers can provide as much or as
little input as they wish: with no input, it is a terse tutorial suitable for experts. With full engagement by
the reader, it becomes an individual tutor. Grant it access to your own work — your writings, your code,
your reading history — and it can gauge your literacy, match the narrative to your style, your technical
background, and calibrate the pace without the need for laborious questionnaires.

An example. Chapter 1 covers the foundations of probability and statistics needed for subsequent
chapters. An ordinary book would pitch this material at some imagined average reader, too tedious for some,
too vague for others. This book presents a compact summary of all the requisite ideas, then asks how you
would like to proceed: by granting access to your own knowledge base (if you do not have one, it will be
built for you as you read), by sharing relevant artifacts, or by answering a few diagnostic questions. The
book then writes a bespoke tutorial at the level of granularity needed to bring you to the threshold required
for subsequent chapters.

At the end of each chapter there is a voluntary questionnaire. You may ignore it, at your own risk. If
you take it and stumble, the book will not simply prompt you to review the same material that evidently
failed you the first time. It will rewrite the material on the fly, based on your errors, to find whatever path
gets you to understanding fastest.

Now let’s get to work.
